## Where Target's official coupons actually live

Target runs its coupon system through its own site and app, so you don't need third-party coupon aggregators to find the real deals. There are four places to check, and they work differently.

**1. The official coupons page (coupons.target.com).** This is Target's digital coupon clipboard — when we checked, it listed 105 active manufacturer-style coupons tied to your Target account. These are brand-specific, category-specific offers, not sitewide codes. A sample of what was live: $2.00 off any Cetaphil or Cetaphil Baby product (trial sizes and single bars excluded), $2.00 off a package of Huggies diapers (not valid on 9-count or smaller), $4.00 off a Neutrogena makeup face product (no makeup removers, trial sizes, or clearance), $1.50 off any size bag of Eight O'Clock coffee, and $0.50 off Huggies wipes 48-count or higher. You "clip" these to your account and they apply automatically when you buy the matching item.

**2. Target Circle offers.** These live in the Target app and your Circle account, and they're the category-wide discounts — percentage-off deals on beauty, grocery, apparel, and so on. Circle is free to join. One perk people overlook: add your birthday to your Circle account and you get a one-time 5% discount on a single purchase on your birthday, redeemed through the app.

**3. The Top Deals page.** This is Target's rotating sale hub — at the moment we looked, it had Halloween candy from $5.99, select costumes at 30% off, pumpkin carving kits BOGO 50% off, indoor furniture up to 50% off (online only), Shark vacuums up to 40% off, and books BOGO 50% off. Target rejects expired codes, codes for items not carried, free-item codes with no purchase requirement, and store-only coupons used online.

One warning about third-party coupon sites: they often list "5% off" style codes that turn out to be the standing Circle Card discount repackaged, or expired offers. The official coupons page and the app's Circle tab are the source of truth.

## The stacking rule that decides everything

Target officially allows three layers of discount per item, and knowing this rule is the difference between a 10% and a 25% savings trip. From Target's own coupon policy: only one manufacturer coupon (paper or digital), one Target coupon (paper or digital), and one Target Circle offer can be combined per item.

So on a single bottle of body wash, you could theoretically use a $2.00 manufacturer coupon, a $1.00 Target coupon, and a 20%-off Circle offer — all on the same item. That's the stack.

The fine print that trips people up:

- **Four identical coupons per household per day**, unless the coupon itself says otherwise.
- **BOGO coupons can't be doubled.** You can't use two buy-one-get-one coupons to get both items free; one item rings up at full price and the second gets the discount.
- **Category and storewide coupons**: one per item per customer, one storewide coupon per transaction, and the purchase threshold is calculated on the item's retail price *before* single-item coupons are applied.
- **"Per purchase" vs "per transaction"** mean different things — "per purchase" means one coupon per item (you can reuse it across multiple items), "per transaction" means once per entire order.
- **Tax is calculated on the full price before a manufacturer coupon is applied**, but Target coupons do reduce the taxable amount.
- **No manufacturer coupons on mobile checkout**, except Target Circle manufacturer coupons.
- **Target coupons and Circle offers don't apply to CVS merchandise** sold inside Target.

Returns work differently too, and this one surprises people: if you return something bought with a manufacturer coupon, the coupon value comes back to you as a Target GiftCard. If you used a Target coupon, the refund is reduced by the coupon value and you don't get that part back.
